Dear Editor,

We wish to submit our original research manuscript, **“Source Heterogeneity, Right Censoring, and Synthetic Augmentation in Fatigue Assessment of LPBF AlSi10Mg,”** for consideration in the above topical collection.

The study addresses a recurring challenge in the fatigue assessment of additively manufactured structural aluminium alloys: published campaigns differ in processing and test conditions, while available fatigue outcomes include both observed failures and right-censored runouts. We assembled a provenance-audited set of 199 unique first exposures and performed three whole-publication-held-out comparisons on 192 specimens at a stress ratio of 0.1. We compared pooled and source-marginal experimental-only Weibull accelerated failure time models with two source-preserving, training-only synthetic augmentation approaches. Explicit censor-aware evaluation showed a failure-versus-runout predictive trade-off, while the tested same-teacher augmentation strategies did not consistently improve performance over the experimental-only source-marginal benchmark. Separately scored experimental campaigns provide bounded transfer observations.

The work is relevant to the collection's interests in fatigue failure, additively manufactured structural metals and data-driven reliability assessment. Its novelty lies in rigorously distinguishing original failure and survival information from dependent retests, repeated sources and synthetic pseudo-observations, and evaluating transfer at the publication level. We explicitly acknowledge that stress amplitude is the only fitted specimen-level predictor and that the current analysis does not identify or measure a new microstructural degradation mechanism.

We believe this cautious, reproducible evaluation can inform the interpretation of fatigue-life modelling in source-heterogeneous LPBF AlSi10Mg datasets and guide future studies combining separately acquired pre-fatigue microstructural descriptors with probabilistic reliability models.
